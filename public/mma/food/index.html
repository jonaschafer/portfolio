The menus

Everything by weight. Tare the cup, pour to the number.

Protein numbers are estimates. Check the labels on your Fairlife and strawberry drinks. "Milk" means regular 2% (about 8 g protein per 250 g). Whey is Isopure unflavored, about 25 g protein a scoop. Everything warm is warm, never hot: your lips are numb and can burn without you feeling it. Test on your inner wrist.

The pour test

How thin is thin enough?

Days 1–7 your bands hold your teeth together, so food has to flow through the gaps. It should run off a spoon in a steady stream, like heavy cream or kefir. If it mounds on the spoon, add 50 g milk and stir.

From about day 8, bands come out to eat, so spoonable thickness (yogurt, pudding) is fine.

Drinkable Greek yogurt

Ratio 1 : 1 by weight

Whisk or shake in a jar. About 20 g protein.

Greek yogurt, plain150 g
Milk150 g
Honey or maple (optional)10 g

Days 1–7: go 1 : 1.5 (150 g yogurt, 225 g milk). Day 8+: 1 : 1, or thicker if you like it. Stir in ¼ scoop whey for +6 g; it disappears in cold yogurt.

Bone broth

One mug = 300 g

About 10 oz. Homemade broth is roughly 6–9 g protein a mug; call it 7.

  1. Put the mug on the scale, tare, pour 300 g.
  2. Microwave until steaming, then let it sit 2 min. Warm, not hot.
  3. Pinch of salt. For a protein boost whisk in 1 scoop collagen (about 9 g more). Isopure says it dissolves in hot liquid too; if it clumps, add it once the broth is warm, not steaming.

Aim for 1–2 mugs a day. It's savory, it counts toward fluid, and it's the break from sweet stuff. Move a freezer container to the fridge the night before; thawed broth keeps about 4 days.

Broth potato soup

The savory one you'll actually want. About 18 g protein with collagen.

Hot bone broth300 g
Instant potato flakes25 g
Butter10 g
Collagen1 scoop

Whisk flakes into the hot broth, rest 1 min, whisk again. Days 1–7 use 15–20 g flakes so it pours. Later, go up to 40 g for a thick soup.

Smoothies

Beast rule

Load in this order: liquid, then yogurt and powder, then frozen fruit on top. Use at least 2 g liquid for every 1 g of frozen fruit. The recipes below are already about 3 : 1. If the blade seizes, add 50 g milk and pulse. Letting frozen fruit sit 10 min on the counter helps a lot.

The Vitamix grinds berry seeds finer than the Beast. For days 1–7 use the Vitamix for berry, or stick to mango, which blends silky. Seeds love braces; rinse after.

Weeks 3–6 (day 22+)

Still the no-chew rule: everything soft enough to mash flat with a fork. Cut or mash first, then swallow.

Soft mac and cheese

Two servings. About 18 g protein each.

Small shells or elbows, dry100 g
Milk250 g
Butter15 g
Flour10 g
Sharp cheddar, grated100 g

Cook pasta 3 min past the box time. Butter and flour in a pot for a minute, whisk in milk until it thickens, melt in the cheese off the heat. Fold in the pasta. Make it saucier than you'd normally want; it tightens as it sits.

Red lentil dal

Four servings of 250 g. About 13 g protein each, plus rice.

Red lentils200 g
Bone broth or water900 g
Onion, grated½
Butter or ghee20 g
Cumin, turmeric, salt1 tsp each

Soften onion in butter with the spices, add lentils and broth, simmer 25 min until the lentils fall apart. Whisk smooth. Rice: cook with an extra ½ cup water so it's very soft. Freezes well.

Polenta + braised beef

Braise once, eat four times. About 26 g protein a serving.

Chuck roast or short rib700 g
Bone broth500 g
Polenta, dry (per serving)35 g
Milk + water (per serving)200 g
Parmesan (per serving)15 g

Brown the beef, cover with broth, lid on, oven at 300°F for 3–4 hours until it shreds with a fork. Shred very fine and mash into the reduced juices. Polenta: whisk into simmering milk and water, cook 20 min, stir in parm. Loose enough to spoon.

If someone's cooking

Blended, freezes well: red lentil (the most protein), potato leek, split pea, butternut squash with ginger, white bean and roasted garlic, blended chicken soup. Use bone broth as the liquid. Freeze in 300 g portions.

Soft, day 10+: chicken congee cooked until it falls apart, baked salmon to flake into potatoes, egg custard or flan, rice pudding, mashed sweet potatoes, cheesy polenta.

Blend completely smooth, serve warm not hot, keep it mild the first two weeks.

Scissors

Keep small scissors on you

If you feel like you're going to vomit, or have any trouble breathing, cut the rubber bands first, then call. Your jaws are held by plates and screws, not the bands. They can always replace them.

Warning signs

Call 911 or go to the ER

  • Trouble breathing, or breathing that's noisy or takes effort
  • Bleeding that doesn't slow after 20 minutes of steady pressure
  • Sudden vision change, or severe pain behind an eye
  • Chest pain, or swelling and pain in one calf

Call the clinic right away

  • Fever of 101.5°F (38.6°C) or higher
  • Your bite suddenly changes, or teeth don't fit the splint grooves
  • Swelling or pain getting worse after day 4
  • Thick yellow or green drainage, or a bad taste or smell that won't go away
  • Vomiting that won't stop, or you can't keep liquids down
  • Urinating much less than normal, or dizzy when you stand
  • Pain your medicine isn't controlling
  • A band, hook, wire, or the splint comes loose and you can't fix it
  • Weight down more than 10 lb, or more than 5%
