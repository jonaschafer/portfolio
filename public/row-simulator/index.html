Inputs

Strokes per minute. The drive takes about the same time at any rate, so this mostly sets how quickly you slide back to the catch.

How far the handle travels from catch to finish. More compression at the catch and a full finish add length.

Average force on the handle during the drive. This is the "pressing hard" part.

The lever on the flywheel. Higher feels heavier and slows the handle, so the drive takes longer. It does not add watts on its own.

How the numbers connect

Work per strokeForce times how far the handle travels.
WattsWork per stroke times strokes per second. Rate multiplies whatever each stroke is worth.
PaceThe PM5 converts watts to pace with a cube root, so doubling watts only makes you about 21% faster.
Meters per strokeBoat speed times the seconds each stroke takes. A higher rate gives each stroke less time to carry you.
Drive and recoveryDrive time comes from length and handle speed. Whatever is left of the stroke is the slide back.

Watts to pace uses Concept2's published formula (watts = 2.80 ÷ (seconds per meter)³). Handle speed, drive time and the damper effect are simplified estimates, so read those as directional.
