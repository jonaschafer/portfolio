My son’s first car

What it buys
The camera squeezeCameras went standard around 2014 on most models, which pushes you into newer years with more miles. Adding one aftermarket ($120 DIY, about $400 installed) opens up 2010 to 2013 cars with fewer miles for the money. Probably the best trade you have.
The real costOregon has no sales tax, but title, registration and dealer fees add a few hundred. At 130k, plan on tires, brakes or fluids soon after. $10k really buys an $8.5k car.
Dealers
Waiting a yearThese cars lose value slowly, so the same money in a year buys about the same car with a few thousand more miles. Don't count on prices dropping.
The readAWD under $10k is very doable. A clean, low-mileage car with a factory camera at that price is rare. Expect 130k+ on a well-kept car and let the inspection decide, or budget closer to $12k for under 100k.

      The math

      Mileage won't be what ends it

      16 to 18, at homeAbout 20k to 25k miles. Buy it at 130k and it's around 155k when he leaves for school.
      If it goes to collegeAnother 5k to 8k a year. Still well under 200k by graduation.
      Budget per year$800 to $1,500 for maintenance and repairs at this age, more in a year it needs tires or brakes.

      What could end it early

      The three real risks

      1. The transmissionThe big one on any car this age. A replacement runs $3k to $7k, most of what the car is worth. Subarus, newer CR-Vs and Nissans use a CVT, which is the bigger risk; the RX 350, CX-5 and older CR-Vs use a regular automatic. Either way, ask for proof the transmission fluid was changed, and walk if it shudders, slips or hesitates on the test drive.
      2. A crashThe first year of driving is by far the riskiest, so this is likely how the car's story ends. It's also the case for an $8k car over a $15k one.
      3. Ordinary age repairsWheel bearings, struts, the AC, small oil leaks. Annoying and a few hundred each, but none of them kill the car.

      In your favor

      Why the good picks hold up

      Timing chainsMost of the good-list engines (Subaru's FB25, Honda's 2.4, Mazda's 2.5, the Lexus V6) use a timing chain, which usually lasts the life of the engine. No $1,000 belt job coming due.
      Bad years filtered outThe model years page already steers you off the known problem years, like Subaru's head gasket era and early oil burners. What's left are the generations with the fewest surprises.
      Portland weatherNo road salt, so rust isn't the killer it is back east.

      The move

      Buy the one with records

      Two identical cars, one with a folder of service receipts and one without: pay the extra $500 for the folder. Records showing transmission fluid and regular oil changes are the best predictor of how long it lasts.

      General patterns for these models and years, not a promise about any one car. A pre-purchase inspection on the actual car beats all of it.

      Timeline

      When to do what

      Permit, around 15Tell your insurer. Most cover permit drivers free, but skipping the call can sink a claim. Ask what his licensed rate will be.
      Driver edTake an ODOT-approved course. It halves the required practice hours to 50, skips the DMV drive test, and earns a discount.
      Shortlisting carsQuote two or three real VINs before buying. Same kid, different car, can mean hundreds a year.
      A month before licenseGet three or more quotes at identical coverage. Your current carrier is usually the one to beat.
      License, 16+Add him to your policy, assigned to his car. Keep the car titled in your name.
      Every renewalRe-shop. Rates fall with each clean year and again at 18 and 25.

      Who to call

      Where to get quotes

      Get one from each. They price teens very differently.

      Your current carrierUsually cheapest thanks to multi-car and bundle discounts. The number to beat.
      Independent agentOne call, several carriers, including Northwest regionals like PEMCO that don't sell direct.
      Direct carriersProgressive, GEICO. Fifteen minutes online, a good check on the agent.
      Captive agentsState Farm, Allstate, Farmers, American Family. Strong teen programs. USAA if anyone in the family served.

      Bring the price down

      Discounts and levers

      Good studentB average or 3.0 GPA, often 10 to 25% off. Send a report card each term.
      Driver trainingThe ODOT course, plus some carriers' own online teen programs.
      Telematics appSnapshot, Drive Safe & Save, Drivewise. Rewards careful driving. Ask if a bad score can raise the rate.
      The car itselfOlder, mid-size, no turbo, cheap to fix. What this tool's grades already favor.
      Deductible$1,000 on his car. If collision costs more than 10% of the car's value a year, drop it.
      The small stuffPay in full, bundle, multi-car. Ask for each by name.
      Later: collegeBig discount if he's 100+ miles away without the car.

      Coverage

      What to carry

      Don't skimp on liability. A teen driver is the biggest lawsuit risk to your house, and higher limits are cheap.

      Oregon minimum25/50/20Plus $15k PIP and 25/50 uninsured motorist. Too thin.
      Carry this100/300/100Or 250/500/100 with real assets. Match uninsured motorist to it.
      Umbrella$1MA few hundred a year. Price it once he's licensed.
      Comp and collisionDependsKeep comprehensive. Collision depends on the car's value.

      Ask every quote

      Questions to ask

      • Is a permit driver covered, and do you need him listed?
      • What's the six-month premium with him added, on this VIN?
      • Which discounts did you apply? Read them back.
      • Is gender a rating factor for you? Most states allow it, and boys pay more.
      • What happens to the rate after his first ticket or at-fault claim?
      • Can the telematics program raise the rate, or only lower it?
      • Does he get assigned to a specific car, and can we choose which?
      • Is there an accident forgiveness option, and what does it cost?
      • When does the rate step down: 17, 18, after three clean years?
      • Umbrella price, and what underlying limits it needs.
